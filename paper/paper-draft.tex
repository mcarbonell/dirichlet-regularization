\begin{abstract}
Weight matrices in modern neural networks are universally initialized and optimized as unstructured, permutation-invariant point clouds. This absence of spatial correlation yields flat, white-noise-like 2D frequency spectra, which severely limits frequency-domain compression and aggressive low-bit quantization. In this work, we introduce \textbf{Dirichlet Weight Regularization} (\texttt{dreg}), a lightweight mathematical framework that incorporates a discrete 2D Dirichlet harmonic energy penalty into standard stochastic gradient optimization. By penalizing spatial differences over a regular 2D lattice, our objective breaks permutation degeneracy and induces continuous, cortical-like weight manifolds with minimal impact on dense task accuracy. Through the exact eigendecomposition of the 2D Neumann graph Laplacian, this spatial continuity penalizes high-frequency modes in the 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (2D-DCT) domain quadratically, concentrating up to $\sim 72\%$ of total Frobenius energy below normalized radius $\rho \le 0.40$ in square attention projections (and $\sim 25\%$ in $4\times$ larger feedforward projections). We pair this spectral compaction with a hierarchical 4-band Base-3 Trit Quantization format (\texttt{.tritq}) built on exact $3^5 = 243 \le 256$ byte packing ($1.60$~bits/trit) with an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ L1-cache lookup table, spanning a configurable rate--distortion frontier from $0.43$~bpp ($74.4\times$) to $3.77$~bpp ($8.5\times$). Empirically, on $10.56$M-parameter autoregressive Transformers trained over $81.92$M tokens of TinyStories, Dirichlet regularization reduces weight roughness by \textbf{72.1\%}, preserves fluent FP32 narrative generation, and improves quantized validation perplexity over unregularized baselines across 5 of 6 rate regimes---including a \textbf{6.76--7.25~PPL advantage} at the calibrated \textbf{1.638~bpp} ($19.5\times$) operating point ($66.50$ vs.\ $73.75$~PPL) and a \textbf{7.02~PPL advantage} at \textbf{2.181~bpp} ($23.08$ vs.\ $30.10$~PPL). Beyond Transformers, in feedforward MNIST classifiers, Dirichlet regularization reduces roughness by \textbf{84.5\%}, nearly doubles low-frequency spectral energy ($27.87\% \to 52.84\%$), improves aggressive low-pass truncation accuracy by \textbf{+10.72\%} absolute ($41.41\%$ vs.\ $30.69\%$), and improves $1.64$~bpp TritQ accuracy by \textbf{+1.19\%}. Finally, we release a portable C11 zero-copy, double-buffered DMA micro-kernel verified to float32 parity ($<10^{-5}$) against PyTorch, and analyze how direct spectral-domain vector-matrix multiplication eliminates full-matrix reconstruction overhead on SRAM-constrained edge devices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Modern deep learning models, particularly Generative Pre-trained Transformers~\citep[GPT;][]{vaswani2017attention}, have achieved remarkable linguistic and perceptual capabilities. However, these capabilities come at substantial memory and energy cost: deploying dense floating-point models on embedded edge hardware, internet-of-things (IoT) microcontrollers, and memory-constrained silicon remains challenging.

To mitigate this bottleneck, post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT) have become central compression paradigms. Methods such as GPTQ~\citep{frantar2022gptq} and AWQ~\citep{lin2023awq} compress models to 4-bit and 3-bit representations by compensating spatial quantization errors, while incoherence-based approaches such as QuIP~\citep{chee2023quip} and QuIP\#~\citep{tseng2024quipsharp} reach 2-bit quantization via randomized Hadamard rotations and lattice codebooks, and native ternary training such as BitNet b1.58~\citep{ma2024bitnet} trains weights directly in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$. Yet frequency-domain compression of neural weight matrices---analogous to JPEG~\citep{wallace1992jpeg} for natural images---has seen limited adoption in dense networks.

We argue that this limitation is a structural consequence of how neural networks are trained. Standard networks are initialized with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) noise and optimized under isotropic $L_2$ weight decay~\citep{loshchilov2017decoupled}. Because $L_2$ regularization is invariant to arbitrary row and column permutations, converged weight matrices exhibit near-zero spatial autocorrelation; their 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) spectra resemble flat white noise (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}). Under spectral truncation or frequency-banded quantization, every frequency bin carries roughly equal variance, causing severe distortion.

In contrast, biological neural substrates minimize wiring length and organize correlated receptive fields into \textbf{topographic cortical maps}~\citep{hubel1962receptive,durbin1990dimension,kohonen1982self}. Neighboring neurons in mammalian visual (V1) and auditory (A1) cortices respond to smoothly varying features over physical two-dimensional tissue.

In this paper, we explore the computational analog of cortical smoothness by introducing \textbf{Dirichlet Weight Regularization} (\texttt{dreg}). Treating each dense weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ as a scalar field over a 2D discrete lattice, we augment the task objective with the discrete \textbf{Dirichlet harmonic energy}, which exerts a Laplace diffusion operator across adjacent weights during backpropagation.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Formulation:} We formulate the discrete 2D Dirichlet penalty on arbitrary linear projections, establish its exact diagonalization in the orthonormal 2D Type-II DCT basis via the Neumann graph Laplacian eigenvalues, and identify how matrix-size normalization ($1/(MN)$) modulates per-weight smoothing across square vs.\ rectangular layers.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Energy Compaction:} We empirically verify that Dirichlet regularization breaks permutation symmetry and concentrates weight variance into low-frequency 2D-DCT harmonics (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}), shifting weight matrices from flat white-noise spectra toward compressible signal-like fields.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq}):} We construct an exact arithmetic byte-packing format exploiting $3^5 = 243 \le 256$ ($1.60$~bits/trit) with a $1.25$~KB L1-cache lookup table and radial frequency banding spanning $0.43$--$3.77$~bpp, while preserving embeddings and output vocabulary heads uncompressed in FP16.
    \item \textbf{Language Modeling Validation on TinyStories-10M:} Across $10.56$M-parameter Transformers trained over $81.92$M tokens, Dirichlet regularization reduces weight roughness by \textbf{72.1\%} and improves quantized perplexity across 5 of 6 rate regimes, including a \textbf{6.76--7.25~PPL gain} at the calibrated $1.638$~bpp ($19.5\times$) operating point and a \textbf{7.02~PPL gain} at $2.181$~bpp ($14.7\times$).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Validation on MNIST MLPs:} Without architecture-specific tuning, \texttt{dreg} reduces spatial roughness by \textbf{84.5\%}, nearly doubles low-frequency energy ($27.87\% \to 52.84\%$), improves low-pass truncation accuracy by \textbf{+10.72\%} absolute ($41.41\%$ vs.\ $30.69\%$), and improves $1.64$~bpp TritQ accuracy by \textbf{+1.19\%}.
    \item \textbf{Portable C11 DMA Micro-Kernel \& Systems Analysis:} We release a zero-copy, double-buffered C11 micro-kernel ($192$~KB working buffers for $128\times128$ matrices, float32 parity $<10^{-5}$ with PyTorch) and derive the algebraic factorization for direct spectral-domain inference $y = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x))$, removing the full-matrix reconstruction bottleneck.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Low-Bit Quantization for Language Models}
Post-training quantization (PTQ) has progressed rapidly to reduce LLM memory footprints. Uniform integer schemes (INT8 and INT4) are widely deployed in production runtimes~\citep{dettmers2022llmint8,xiao2023smoothquant}. Second-order error compensation was scaled to billion-parameter Transformers by GPTQ~\citep{frantar2022gptq}, while AWQ~\citep{lin2023awq} protects salient activation channels and SqueezeLLM~\citep{kim2023squeezellm} combines sensitivity-aware non-uniform clustering with sparse outlier extraction.

At the $\le 2$~bpp frontier, QuIP~\citep{chee2023quip} and QuIP\#~\citep{tseng2024quipsharp} apply randomized orthogonal/Hadamard transforms to enforce incoherence before 2-bit scalar or $E_8$ lattice quantization. Complementary PTQ methods---SpQR~\citep{dettmers2023spqr}, OmniQuant~\citep{shao2024omniquant}, BiLLM~\citep{huang2024billm}, and PB-LLM~\citep{shang2024pbllm}---push toward 1--2~bpp via sparse outliers, learned clipping, or partial binarization, while QAT frameworks such as BitNet b1.58~\citep{ma2024bitnet} train ternary weights end-to-end. Notably, incoherence methods like QuIP\# \emph{destroy} spatial correlation to flatten weight distributions, whereas our work explores the complementary opposite: \emph{inducing} 2D spatial smoothness during training so that weights compact naturally in the orthonormal 2D-DCT domain.

\subsection{Topographic Mappings in Neuroscience and Machine Learning}
Biological cortical topography was classically characterized in orientation columns~\citep{hubel1962receptive}, modeled computationally via Self-Organizing Maps~\citep{kohonen1982self}, and analyzed as wiring-length-minimizing dimension reduction~\citep{durbin1990dimension}. In deep learning, Topographic VAEs~\citep{keller2021topographic} learn equivariant capsules on 2D grids, and spectral-norm regularization~\citep{yoshida2017spectral} constrains singular values for generalization, but neither connects 2D weight-lattice smoothness to DCT energy compaction and frequency-banded quantization.

\subsection{Spectral Transforms in Deep Learning}
The Discrete Cosine Transform~\citep{ahmed1974dct} underpins JPEG~\citep{wallace1992jpeg} due to its orthonormality and near-optimal energy compaction for Markov-correlated signals~\citep{strang1999dct}. Gaussian-process theory~\citep{rasmussen2006gp} likewise predicts power-law spectral decay under Laplacian priors. In deep learning, Fourier-domain parameterizations have recently been applied to parameter-efficient fine-tuning~\citep[FourierFT;][]{gao2024fourierft}. Applying DCT directly to standard neural network weights, however, yields poor compression because standard weights have flat spectra; our work investigates 2D Dirichlet regularization as a lightweight training-time inductive bias to induce DCT compressibility.

\section{Methodology: Dirichlet Regularization \& Spectral Quantization}
\label{sec:method}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{From Isotropic Weight Decay to 2D Dirichlet Harmonic Surface Tension}
Standard regularized training minimizes a task loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}$ under $L_2$ weight decay:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{W} \; \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(W; \mathcal{D}) + \frac{\gamma}{2} \|W\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
where $\|W\|_F^2 = \sum_{i,j} W_{i,j}^2$. Because the Frobenius norm is strictly invariant under arbitrary row permutations $\pi$ and column permutations $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
    \|\pi(W)\sigma(W)\|_F^2 = \|W\|_F^2,
\end{equation}
standard optimization places no coupling between adjacent entries $W_{i,j}$ and $W_{i,j+1}$, leaving converged weight variance uniformly dispersed across spatial frequencies.

Motivated by the continuous Dirichlet energy functional $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{W}) = \frac{1}{2} \int_\Omega \|\nabla \mathcal{W}(u, v)\|^2 \, du \, dv$ over a 2D cortical sheet $\Omega$, we define the normalized discrete 2D Dirichlet energy directly on the unit-spacing $M \times N$ lattice $\mathbb{Z}_M \times \mathbb{Z}_N$ (avoiding aspect-ratio distortion factors $M/N$ and $N/M$ that arise when discretizing onto $[0,1]^2$ with $\Delta u = 1/M, \Delta v = 1/N$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}(W) = \frac{1}{2 M N} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{M-1} \sum_{j=1}^N (W_{i+1, j} - W_{i, j})^2 + \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} (W_{i, j+1} - W_{i, j})^2 \right].
    \label{eq:dirichlet_discrete}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in matrix-trace form using the 1D Neumann path-graph Laplacians $L_M \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$ and $L_N \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}(W)
    &= \frac{1}{2 M N} \Big( \operatorname{Tr}(W^\top L_M W) + \operatorname{Tr}(W L_N W^\top) \Big) \\
    &= \frac{1}{2 M N} \operatorname{vec}(W)^\top (I_N \otimes L_M + L_N \otimes I_M) \operatorname{vec}(W).
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:dirichlet_trace}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Normalization Conventions.} The \texttt{dreg} library exposes two normalized conventions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{numel}: $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{numel}}(W) = \frac{1}{MN}\sum \Delta^2 = 2\,\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}(W)$. Used during TinyStories-10M training (Tables~\ref{tab:calibration} and~\ref{tab:10k_results}) and MNIST MLP evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:mnist}).
    \item \texttt{edges}: $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{edges}}(W) = \frac{1}{(M-1)N + M(N-1)}\sum \Delta^2 \approx \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}(W)$. Used in post-hoc Pareto evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:pareto_sweep}), annealing PoC (\S\ref{sec:annealing}), and GPT-2 diagnostics.
\end{itemize}
Because $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{numel}} \approx 2\,\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{edges}}$ for large matrices, both measure identical relative roughness reductions, while reported absolute values differ by $\approx 2\times$; we label the convention explicitly in every table.

Across all $L$ regularized linear projections $\{W^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^L$ (explicitly excluding token embeddings and the output vocabulary head \texttt{lm\_head}, whose token indices have no spatial topology), the training objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\mathcal{W}; \mathcal{D}) + \frac{\lambda_{\text{topo}}}{L} \sum_{l=1}^L \mathcal{E}_D(W^{(l)}).
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
For an interior entry $(i, j)$ under $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}$, the negative gradient is the discrete 5-point Laplace diffusion stencil (with Neumann reflection at the borders):
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{\partial \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{var}}}{\partial W_{i, j}} = \frac{1}{MN} [\Delta W]_{i, j} = \frac{1}{MN} \left( W_{i+1, j} + W_{i-1, j} + W_{i, j+1} + W_{i, j-1} - 4 W_{i, j} \right),
\end{equation}
with an additional factor of $2$ under \texttt{numel}. Notice an important scaling property: because each layer's energy is normalized by $MN$, larger matrices (such as $256 \times 1024$ FFN projections) experience a $4\times$ weaker per-weight Dirichlet gradient relative to the task gradient than smaller square matrices ($256 \times 256$ attention projections)---an asymmetry we observe directly in Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}.

\subsection{Spectral Energy Concentration via the 2D-DCT}
The eigenvectors of the 1D Neumann path-graph Laplacian $L_N$ are precisely the orthonormal Type-II Discrete Cosine Transform basis vectors~\citep{strang1999dct}:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k(n) = \alpha_k \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi (2n+1) k}{2N}\right), \quad \alpha_k = \begin{cases} \sqrt{1/N}, & k = 0, \\ \sqrt{2/N}, & k = 1, \dots, N-1. \end{cases}
    \label{eq:dct_basis}
\end{equation}
By tensor product, the separable 2D-DCT maps $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ to its spectral coefficients $S \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$:
\begin{equation}
    S = D_M W D_N^\top, \quad W = D_M^\top S D_N,
\end{equation}
where $D_M^\top D_M = I_M$ and $D_N^\top D_N = I_N$. By Parseval's theorem, $\|W\|_F^2 = \|S\|_F^2$, while the discrete Dirichlet energy diagonalizes exactly (under the \texttt{numel} convention):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{numel}}(W) = \frac{1}{MN} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \mu_{k, l} S_{k, l}^2, \quad \mu_{k, l} = 4 \left[ \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{2M}\right) + \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi l}{2N}\right) \right].
    \label{eq:spectral_eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
Because $\mu_{k,l} \approx \pi^2 \big((k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2\big)$ grows quadratically with normalized spatial frequency, penalizing $\mathcal{E}_D(W)$ suppresses high-frequency coefficients $S_{k,l}^2$ with quadratic severity, connecting our objective to Laplacian eigenmaps~\citep{belkin2003laplacian}. Under a Gaussian prior $p(W) \propto \exp(-\frac{\lambda}{2}[\operatorname{Tr}(W^\top L_M W) + \operatorname{Tr}(W L_N W^\top)])$~\citep{rasmussen2006gp}, this motivates power-law spectral decay $\mathbb{E}[|S_{u,v}|^2] \propto (1 + \lambda(u^2 + v^2))^{-1}$ (used as field-theoretic intuition rather than a dynamical theorem for AdamW).

\subsection{Hierarchical Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq})}
\label{sec:tritq}
Capitalizing on spectral compaction, we partition the 2D frequency grid $(u, v) = (k/M, l/N) \in [0, 1)^2$ by normalized radial frequency $\rho(u, v) = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2} \in [0, \sqrt{2})$ into four hierarchical bands controlled by radii $0 \le r_0 \le r_1 \le r_2$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Band 0 ($\rho \le r_0$):} DC and basal harmonics, quantized to 8-bit unsigned integers (\texttt{uint8}) with per-band affine scaling ($8.0$~bpp).
    \item \textbf{Band 1 ($r_0 < \rho \le r_1$):} Mid-frequency harmonics, quantized to 4-bit nibbles packed 2 per byte ($4.0$~bpp).
    \item \textbf{Band 2 ($r_1 < \rho \le r_2$):} High-mid harmonics, quantized to balanced ternary trits $t \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ using threshold $\tau = 0.5\sigma_2$ and reconstruction scale $\sigma_2$, packed via Base-3 arithmetic ($1.60$~bits/trit).
    \item \textbf{Band 3 ($\rho > r_2$):} High-frequency tail, truncated to zero and omitted from storage ($0.0$~bpp).
\end{itemize}
Because band occupancy scales with the radial cutoffs, $(r_0, r_1, r_2)$ smoothly sweeps the rate--distortion frontier (Table~\ref{tab:pareto_sweep}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Calibrated operating point (TinyStories-10M \& MNIST):} With cutoffs $(r_0, r_1, r_2) = (0.15, 0.40, 1.00)$, Band~0 holds $\sim 1.8\%$ of coefficients, Band~1 $\sim 10.9\%$, Band~2 $\sim 66.1\%$, and Band~3 truncates $\sim 21.2\%$, giving an effective linear-weight bitrate of \textbf{1.638~bpp} ($19.5\times$ vs.\ FP32; $1.68$~MB across all 56 linear matrices of TinyStories-10M).
    \item \textbf{Default ultra-aggressive library preset:} With $(r_0, r_1, r_2) = (0.10, 0.25, 0.50)$, Bands~0--2 hold $\sim 0.8\% / 4.2\% / 14.8\%$ and Band~3 truncates $\sim 80.3\%$, yielding \textbf{0.468~bpp} ($68.3\times$ compression).
\end{itemize}
In serialized \texttt{.tritq} checkpoints, non-topographic parameters (1D LayerNorms, positional embeddings, token embeddings, and \texttt{lm\_head}) are stored uncompressed in FP16 so that vocabulary geometry is never distorted by spectral truncation.

\paragraph{Exact Base-3 Byte Packing and $\mathcal{O}(1)$ LUT Unpacking.}
Storing ternary values $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ with 2 bits wastes $25\%$ of states. Observing that $3^5 = 243 \le 256 = 2^8$, we bijectively pack five trits $(t_0, \dots, t_4)$ into one unsigned byte $B \in [0, 242]$:
\begin{equation}
    B = \sum_{p=0}^{4} 3^p (t_p + 1), \quad \text{Bitrate}_{\text{trit}} = \frac{8 \text{ bits}}{5 \text{ trits}} = 1.60 \text{ bits/trit},
\end{equation}
saving $20\%$ storage relative to 2-bit encoding. Unpacking requires no division or branching via a static precomputed table $\text{LUT} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}^{256 \times 5}$ occupying $1.25$~KB in L1 data cache.

\paragraph{Reconstruction Complexity and the Direct Spectral-Domain Factorization.}
When reconstructing a full $D \times D$ matrix explicitly via dense matrix multiplication $W = D^\top S D$, two GEMMs require $4D^3$ FLOPs (or $\mathcal{O}(D^2 \log D)$ via fast 2D-IDCT) and $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ scratchpad memory. For $D = 128$, the working buffers (\texttt{dct\_buf}, \texttt{temp\_buf}, and ping-pong \texttt{target\_buf}) occupy $256$~KB, fitting easily inside $512$~KB--$1$~MB microcontroller SRAM. However, for wide layers such as TinyStories-10M FFNs ($256 \times 1024$, $1.0$~MB per FP32 matrix), explicit full-matrix reconstruction requires $\ge 4$~MB of working buffers plus embedding tables.

Two architectural paths eliminate this bottleneck:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Block-DCT Tiling:} Partitioning weights into fixed $B \times B$ blocks (e.g., $64 \times 64$, provided in \texttt{dreg.BlockDCTTiler}) bounds per-tile reconstruction cost to $\mathcal{O}(B^3)$ and per-tile scratchpad to $3 B^2 \cdot 4\text{ B} = 48$~KB, independent of $D$.
    \item \textbf{Direct Spectral-Domain Matrix-Vector Product:} Crucially, in single-token autoregressive decoding ($B=1, T=1$), explicit reconstruction of $W = D_M^\top S D_N$ is algebraically unnecessary. Associating from left to right:
    \begin{equation}
        y = x W^\top = x (D_N^\top S^\top D_M) = \big((x D_N^\top) S^\top\big) D_M = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}\!\Big(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x)\Big),
        \label{eq:spectral_matvec}
    \end{equation}
    the forward pass requires only a 1D-DCT of vector $x$ ($\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$), a streaming dot-product with the nonzero bands of $S$ unpacked on the fly from Flash ($\le 2 f_{\text{kept}} M N$ FLOPs---fewer operations than dense GEMM), and a 1D-IDCT of the output ($\mathcal{O}(M \log M)$). This reduces active working SRAM from $\mathcal{O}(MN)$ to $\mathcal{O}(M + N)$ (e.g., $5$~KB for $256 \times 1024$). Integrating direct spectral streaming into our C kernel is a primary target for future systems releases.
\end{enumerate}

\caption{Dirichlet Regularized Training and Calibrated Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq})}
\label{alg:dreg_pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Dataset $\mathcal{D}$, regularized linear weights $\mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}} = \{W^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^L$ (excluding embeddings and \texttt{lm\_head}), learning rate $\eta$, Dirichlet coupling $\lambda_{\text{topo}}$, radial cutoffs $(r_0, r_1, r_2)$ (calibrated: $(0.15, 0.40, 1.00)$).
\STATE \textbf{Phase 1: Harmonic Regularized Optimization}
\FOR{step $t = 1$ \TO $T$}
    \STATE Sample minibatch $(X, Y) \sim \mathcal{D}$ and compute task loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(X, Y; \mathcal{W})$.
    \STATE Initialize $\mathcal{E}_D \leftarrow 0$.
    \FOR{each $W \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}}$ of shape $M \times N$}
        \STATE Compute finite differences $\Delta_x W_{i,j} = W_{i+1,j} - W_{i,j}$ and $\Delta_y W_{i,j} = W_{i,j+1} - W_{i,j}$.
        \STATE $\mathcal{E}_D \leftarrow \mathcal{E}_D + \frac{1}{MN} \left( \|\Delta_x W\|_F^2 + \|\Delta_y W\|_F^2 \right)$ \quad (\texttt{numel} convention).
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \frac{\lambda_{\text{topo}}}{L} \mathcal{E}_D$.
    \STATE Update all parameters $\mathcal{W} \leftarrow \text{AdamW}(\mathcal{W}, \nabla_{\mathcal{W}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}, \eta)$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Phase 2: Post-Training Hierarchical Base-3 Spectral Quantization}
\FOR{each $W \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}}$}
    \STATE Transform to 2D spectral domain: $S \leftarrow D_M W D_N^\top$ via orthonormal 2D-DCT.
    \STATE Compute normalized radial frequencies $\rho(k, l) = \sqrt{(k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2}$.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 0 ($\rho \le r_0$):} Affine 8-bit \texttt{uint8} quantization.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 1 ($r_0 < \rho \le r_1$):} Affine 4-bit nibble packing (2 values/byte).
    \STATE \textbf{Band 2 ($r_1 < \rho \le r_2$):} Ternary trits $t \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ packed 5/byte via $B = \sum_{p=0}^4 3^p (t_p + 1)$.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 3 ($\rho > r_2$):} Truncate to zero (0 bits stored).
\ENDFOR
\STATE Serialize non-topographic parameters (embeddings, \texttt{lm\_head}, norms) in FP16.
\RETURN Compressed \texttt{.tritq} checkpoint.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Discussion \& Analysis}
\label{sec:discussion}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Why External Template Anchoring Fails: The Rank-1 Clamping Attractor}
During early exploratory work, we tested whether cross-seed alignment could be induced via an external harmonic potential $R_{\text{ext}}(W) = -\lambda \sum \sin(2\pi i / M) W_{i, j}$. While this forced independent seeds to correlate strongly ($\rho_{\text{raw}} = 0.996$), it triggered a catastrophic \textbf{rank-1 clamping attractor}: the effective singular rank collapsed from $199.2$ to $3.58$, destroying expressivity. This negative result clarifies that \textbf{the value of spatial regularization lies in intrinsic self-organization, not external template clamping}: intrinsic Dirichlet diffusion preserves full representational rank while compacting energy toward low frequencies.

\subsection{Hardware and Edge Silicon Implications}
Our C11 DMA micro-kernel demonstrates zero-copy double-buffered spectral unpacking with float32 parity ($3.6 \times 10^{-7}$ max error, $192$~KB working buffers for $128 \times 128$ matrices). For compact edge models ($d_{\text{model}} \le 128$), explicit ping-pong decompression fits within $512$~KB--$1$~MB microcontroller SRAM (e.g., STM32H7, ESP32-S3, Cortex-M55). Scaling sub-megabyte SRAM execution to wider 10M+ models ($d_{\text{ffn}} = 1024$) requires avoiding full-matrix materialization via Block-DCT tiling or the direct spectral-domain matvec $y = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x))$ derived in \S\ref{sec:systems}, alongside Flash-streamed embedding lookups.

\subsection{Topological Annealing: Preliminary Proof-of-Concept}
\label{sec:annealing}
Static Dirichlet regularization introduces a small dense FP32 gap ($5.83 \to 6.18$~PPL in Table~\ref{tab:10k_results}). To investigate whether releasing surface tension late in training recovers dense capacity while retaining smoothness via geometric inertia, we implemented \textbf{Dirichlet Annealing} (\texttt{StepCooldownScheduler}):
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_{\text{topo}}(t) = 
    \begin{cases}
        \lambda_0 & \text{for } t < (1 - \rho_{\text{cooldown}}) T, \\
        0 & \text{for } t \ge (1 - \rho_{\text{cooldown}}) T.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Preliminary 5k-Step PoC and Confounds.}
In an exploratory 5,000-step TinyStories-10M run ($\lambda_0 = 30.0$, $\rho_{\text{cooldown}} = 0.15$, using \texttt{edges} normalization), releasing $\lambda \to 0$ at step $4{,}250$ immediately improved dense FP32 perplexity from $7.90$ to $7.50$~PPL while retaining $E_D^{\text{edges}} = 0.002208$ ($-64.3\%$ relative to the 10k Standard baseline $0.006178$) and achieving $55.68$~PPL at $1.638$~bpp. \textbf{Important methodological caveat:} this PoC is \emph{not} a controlled comparison against Table~\ref{tab:pareto_sweep} ($66.50$~PPL at 10k steps) for two reasons: (1) shorter training runs have lower weight norms and lower quantized PPL even without annealing (compare $39.80$~PPL at 2k steps in Table~\ref{tab:calibration} vs.\ $66.13$~PPL at 10k steps in Table~\ref{tab:10k_results}), and (2) the 5k run used \texttt{edges} normalization ($\sim 0.5\times$ the effective $\lambda$ of the 10k \texttt{numel} run). A matched 10k-step controlled run with identical normalization is required to verify whether annealing strictly improves the 10k Pareto frontier.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Several important limitations of this study and preprint warrant transparent discussion:

\paragraph{Single-Seed Evaluation.}
All TinyStories-10M and MNIST results reported in Tables~\ref{tab:calibration}--\ref{tab:pareto_sweep} are from single training runs ($N=1$ seed). While the $72\%$--$85\%$ roughness reduction and rate--distortion trends are large and consistent across 5 of 6 bit-rates, multi-seed replications ($N \ge 3$) with mean $\pm$ standard deviation are needed for formal statistical significance testing.

\paragraph{Scope of Comparative Baselines.}
Table~\ref{tab:sota} compares \texttt{.tritq} against per-tensor uniform RTN baselines (INT4/INT2). We have not yet benchmarked against per-group RTN, second-order PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP\#), native low-bit QAT (BitNet b1.58), quantized low-rank SVD at matched byte budgets, or competing regularizers (heavy $L_2$ weight decay sweeps, Total Variation $L_1$). These comparisons are necessary before claiming overall superiority over established PTQ/QAT pipelines.

\paragraph{Layer-Size Scaling Asymmetry ($1/(MN)$ Attenuation).}
As revealed in Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}, normalizing each matrix's Dirichlet energy by $MN$ attenuates per-weight smoothing by $4\times$ on $256 \times 1024$ FFN projections ($\sim 25\%$ energy below $\rho = 0.40$) relative to $256 \times 256$ Attention projections ($\sim 72\%$). Scale-balanced or variance-normalized penalties $\mathcal{E}_D(W) / \|W\|_F^2$, together with MSE-optimal ternary reconstruction scaling $\alpha^* = \mathbb{E}[|s| \mid |s| > \tau]$ in Band~2, are expected to substantially improve low-bit perplexity.

\paragraph{Pre-Trained Model Adaptation (GPT-2 124M).}
Evaluating pre-trained OpenAI GPT-2 (124M, 48 dense projections) confirms that unregularized pre-trained weights exhibit high intrinsic roughness ($E_D^{\text{edges}} = 0.033514$) and collapse under $1.63$~bpp \texttt{.tritq} ($8{,}055.96$~PPL vs.\ $14.31$~FP32). In an initial 300-step fine-tuning test, a small penalty ($\lambda = 10^{-3}$) paired with a misconfigured early cooldown (active for only 45 steps) produced negligible roughness change ($\Delta E_D < 0.01\%$). Exploratory 2D Bipartite TSP row/column permutation reduced GPT-2 roughness by $13.8\%$ and halved $1.63$~bpp perplexity ($8{,}055.96 \to 4{,}374.83$~PPL), though still collapsed. Properly calibrated high-$\lambda$ fine-tuning ($\lambda \in [10, 100]$) and spectral PEFT adapters~\citep{gao2024fourierft} for pre-trained LLMs remain open work.

\paragraph{Training Overhead and Planar Topology.}
Evaluating the 2D finite-difference stencil adds $\mathcal{O}(MN)$ work per regularized layer during training (typically $<5\%$ wall-clock overhead on GPU). Our formulation also assumes a planar 2D lattice; non-Euclidean (toroidal, spherical, or learned graph-Laplacian) topologies remain unexplored.

\section{Broader Impact}
\label{sec:broader_impact}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reducing the storage and memory bandwidth demands of neural networks helps enable private, offline intelligence on low-power edge devices and lowers operational energy consumption. Because the models studied here are small ($235\text{K}$--$10.56\text{M}$ parameters), dual-use risks are minimal relative to frontier systems; nevertheless, aggressive quantization can alter model calibration and error profiles, so compressed models should be audited carefully before deployment in safety-critical settings.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented Dirichlet Weight Regularization (\texttt{dreg}), a simple training-time penalty that breaks permutation invariance in neural weight matrices to induce smooth 2D manifolds and concentrate spectral energy in the orthonormal 2D-DCT domain. Paired with Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq}), \texttt{dreg} reduces weight roughness by $72.1\%$ on TinyStories-10M and $84.5\%$ on MNIST MLPs, delivering consistent rate--distortion improvements across the $1.64$--$3.77$~bpp frontier ($+7.25$~PPL at $1.638$~bpp, $+7.02$~PPL at $2.181$~bpp) and $+10.72\%$ higher accuracy under aggressive low-pass truncation on MNIST. Together with our portable C11 DMA micro-kernel and direct spectral-domain matvec analysis, these results establish spatial weight geometry as a promising, under-explored axis for compact neural network design.

\section{Continuous Gradient Flow Motivation}
\label{sec:appendix_derivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let $\mathcal{W}: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ denote a continuously differentiable scalar field over a compact domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$. The continuous Dirichlet energy functional is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{W}) = \frac{1}{2} \int_\Omega \|\nabla \mathcal{W}(u, v)\|^2 \, du \, dv = \frac{1}{2} \int_\Omega \left( \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{W}}{\partial u}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{W}}{\partial v}\right)^2 \right) du \, dv.
\end{equation}
Taking the first variation with respect to a smooth test perturbation $\eta$:
\begin{align}
    \left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon} \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{W} + \epsilon \eta)\right|_{\epsilon=0} &= \int_\Omega \nabla \mathcal{W} \cdot \nabla \eta \, du \, dv \\
    &= \int_{\partial \Omega} \eta (\nabla \mathcal{W} \cdot \hat{n}) \, ds - \int_\Omega \eta \Delta \mathcal{W} \, du \, dv.
\end{align}
Under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions $\nabla \mathcal{W} \cdot \hat{n} = 0$ on $\partial \Omega$ (zero normal flux at the matrix borders), the boundary term vanishes, yielding the Euler--Lagrange gradient $-\Delta \mathcal{W}$ and the continuous heat diffusion flow:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{W}}{\partial t} = -\frac{\delta \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}}{\delta \mathcal{W}} = \Delta \mathcal{W}.
\end{equation}
On the unit-spacing discrete lattice $\mathbb{Z}_M \times \mathbb{Z}_N$, the corresponding discrete energy~\eqref{eq:dirichlet_discrete} produces the exact 5-point Neumann Laplacian stencil at interior coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    [\Delta W]_{i, j} = W_{i+1, j} + W_{i-1, j} + W_{i, j+1} + W_{i, j-1} - 4 W_{i, j},
\end{equation}
acting as an intrinsic spatial low-pass diffusion operator during optimization.
