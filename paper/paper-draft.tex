\begin{abstract}
Weight matrices in modern neural networks are universally initialized and optimized as unstructured, permutation-invariant point clouds. This absence of spatial correlation yields flat, white-noise-like 2D frequency spectra, which severely limits frequency-domain compression and aggressive sub-2-bit quantization. We introduce \textbf{Dirichlet Weight Regularization} (\texttt{dreg}), a lightweight training-time framework that imposes a discrete 2D Dirichlet harmonic energy penalty over each linear projection's natural 2D lattice. By diagonalizing the 2D Neumann graph Laplacian in the orthonormal 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (2D-DCT) basis, our penalty suppresses high spatial frequencies quadratically. Crucially, we show that naive element-count normalization ($1/(MN)$) starves wide feedforward layers ($4\times$ weaker per-weight smoothing than square attention), and resolve this via \textbf{scale-invariant Riemannian Rayleigh normalization} ($\mathcal{R}(W) = \sum \Delta^2 / (2\|W\|_F^2)$) with \textbf{parameter-weighted layer aggregation}---lifting low-frequency energy compaction ($\rho \le 0.40$) to \textbf{91.6\%} (Attention) and \textbf{94.2\%} (FFN) simultaneously (vs.\ $12.6\%$ / $12.9\%$ unregularized). Paired with our hierarchical 4-band \textbf{Base-3 Trit Quantization} format (\texttt{.tritq}, $3^5 = 243 \le 256$ byte packing at $1.60$~bits/trit) enhanced with zero-overhead \textbf{MSE-optimal Band-0/1 clipping} and \textbf{1D Lloyd--Max Band-2 ternary quantization}, multi-seed ($N=3$) 10,000-step experiments on TinyStories-10M ($81.92$M tokens) demonstrate that controlled Dirichlet annealing ($\lambda = 2.0 \to 0$) strictly Pareto-dominates static regularization and unregularized baselines: at \textbf{1.638~bpp} ($19.5\times$ compression), it achieves \textbf{16.87 $\pm$ 1.73~PPL} vs.\ $68.21 \pm 6.94$ for Standard TritQ (\textbf{4.04$\times$ lower PPL}), outperforming Spatial Group-wise INT2 ($g=64$, $2.25$~bpp, $36.91 \pm 6.03$) by \textbf{2.19$\times$} at \textbf{27\% fewer bits} and budget-matched INT8 SVD ($555.65 \pm 65.74$) by \textbf{32.9$\times$}. At \textbf{2.181~bpp} ($14.7\times$), perplexity drops to \textbf{10.71 $\pm$ 0.64} (only $+2.93$ above FP32), while at sub-1-bit \textbf{0.914~bpp} ($35.0\times$), it cuts perplexity \textbf{11.4$\times$} ($95.60 \pm 28.26$ vs.\ $1{,}085.5 \pm 214.1$). We further derive the AdamW gradient scaling law for pre-trained LLMs and show that 1,500-step stop-gradient Rayleigh adaptation on pre-trained \textbf{OpenAI GPT-2 (124M)} cuts weight roughness by \textbf{95.6\%} and reduces compressed perplexity by \textbf{23$\times$--77$\times$} across $1.63$--$3.77$~bpp (e.g., $21.62$ vs.\ $684.46$ at $3.77$~bpp; $60.35$ vs.\ $4{,}051.0$ at $2.18$~bpp). Cross-architecture MNIST MLP validation confirms \textbf{$-84.5\%$} roughness and \textbf{+10.72\%} low-pass truncation accuracy. Finally, our portable C11 micro-kernel implements direct spectral matrix-vector multiplication $y = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x))$, slashing TinyStories-10M FFN working SRAM from $8{,}448$~KB to \textbf{13.0~KB} (\textbf{650$\times$} reduction) and running \textbf{$\sim 45\times$ faster} than full-matrix reconstruction at float32 parity ($8.9 \times 10^{-7}$ max error).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Modern deep learning models, particularly Generative Pre-trained Transformers~\citep[GPT;][]{vaswani2017attention}, have achieved remarkable linguistic and perceptual capabilities, yet deploying dense floating-point weights on embedded edge hardware, microcontrollers, and bandwidth-limited silicon remains a primary bottleneck.

Post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT) have made rapid strides toward extreme compression. Second-order error compensation such as GPTQ~\citep{frantar2022gptq} and activation-aware scaling such as AWQ~\citep{lin2023awq} preserve quality down to 3--4 bits, while incoherence-based schemes such as QuIP~\citep{chee2023quip} and QuIP\#~\citep{tseng2024quipsharp} apply randomized Hadamard rotations to reach 2-bit lattice quantization, and native ternary QAT such as BitNet b1.58~\citep{ma2024bitnet} trains weights directly in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$. Yet frequency-domain compression of neural weight matrices---analogous to JPEG~\citep{wallace1992jpeg} for natural images---has seen little adoption in dense networks.

We argue that this limitation stems directly from how neural networks are trained. Standard networks are initialized with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) noise and optimized under isotropic $L_2$ weight decay~\citep{loshchilov2017decoupled}. Because $L_2$ regularization is invariant to arbitrary row and column permutations, converged weight matrices exhibit near-zero spatial autocorrelation; their 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (2D-DCT) spectra resemble flat white noise (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}), leaving only $\sim 12.6\%$ of total Frobenius energy below normalized frequency $\rho = 0.40$.

In contrast, biological neural substrates minimize wiring length and organize correlated receptive fields into \textbf{topographic cortical maps}~\citep{hubel1962receptive,durbin1990dimension,kohonen1982self}, where neighboring neurons in visual (V1) and auditory (A1) cortices vary smoothly across two-dimensional tissue.

We introduce \textbf{Dirichlet Weight Regularization} (\texttt{dreg}), which treats each dense weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ as a scalar field over a discrete 2D lattice and penalizes its discrete \textbf{Dirichlet harmonic energy} during backpropagation. Beyond formulating the basic penalty, we uncover and solve three subtle geometric and quantization pathologies that previously bottlenecked spectral weight compression:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Eliminating FFN Starvation via Riemannian Rayleigh + Parameter-Weighted Normalization:} We prove that per-matrix $1/(MN)$ normalization attenuates per-weight smoothing by $4\times$ on rectangular $256\times1024$ FFNs relative to $256\times256$ Attention layers (stagnating FFN compaction at $21.7\%$ vs.\ $54.5\%$, Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}). Replacing it with the scale-invariant Rayleigh quotient $\mathcal{R}(W) = \sum \Delta^2 / (2\|W\|_F^2)$ (whose gradient is the exact Riemannian gradient tangent to the Frobenius sphere, $\langle W, \nabla_W \mathcal{R}(W)\rangle = 0$) and parameter-weighted layer aggregation raises \emph{both} Attention and FFN compaction above $91\%$--$98\%$. We further contrast full Riemannian Rayleigh (optimal from scratch) against stop-gradient Rayleigh (\texttt{rayleigh\_sg}, optimal for damping oversized outliers in pre-trained LLMs).
    \item \textbf{Zero-Bit-Overhead Optimal Spectral Quantization (\texttt{.tritq}):} Building on exact Base-3 byte packing ($3^5 = 243 \le 256 \Rightarrow 1.60$~bits/trit, $1.25$~KB L1-cache LUT), we replace heuristic Band-2 thresholding and unclipped Band-0/1 \texttt{min}--\texttt{max} scaling with \textbf{1D Lloyd--Max MSE-optimal ternary quantization} and \textbf{MSE-optimal asymmetric clipping} on Bands~0 and~1. Without adding a single bit to the \texttt{.tritq} v1 bitstream or modifying the C11 decoder, this reduces $1.638$~bpp perplexity by \textbf{3.06$\times$} ($51.55 \to 16.87$~PPL).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Seed ($N=3$) TinyStories-10M Validation \& Controlled 10k Annealing:} Across 12 parallel 10,000-step A10G runs ($81.92$M tokens each, seeds $\{42, 100, 2024\}$), controlled Rayleigh annealing ($\lambda = 2.0 \to 0$, \texttt{cooldown\_ratio} $= 0.15$) strictly Pareto-dominates static regularization at every bit-rate: achieving \textbf{16.87 $\pm$ 1.73~PPL} at \textbf{1.638~bpp} ($19.5\times$, beating Standard TritQ $68.21$ by \textbf{4.04$\times$}, Group-wise INT2 $g=64$ at $2.25$~bpp $36.91$ by \textbf{2.19$\times$} using 27\% fewer bits, and INT8 SVD $555.65$ by \textbf{32.9$\times$}), \textbf{10.71 $\pm$ 0.64~PPL} at \textbf{2.181~bpp}, and \textbf{95.60 $\pm$ 28.26~PPL} at sub-1-bit \textbf{0.914~bpp} ($35.0\times$, \textbf{11.4$\times$} better than Standard).
    \item \textbf{Pre-Trained LLM Adaptation (OpenAI GPT-2 124M):} Deriving the AdamW gradient scaling law ($\lambda_{\text{eff}} \propto N_{\text{total}} \sigma_W$), we show that 1,500 steps of calibrated \texttt{rayleigh\_sg} adaptation across all 48 dense \texttt{Conv1D} projections ($84.9$M weights) cuts roughness by \textbf{95.6\%}, boosts compaction to $73.0\%$ / $79.2\%$, and lowers compressed GPT-2 perplexity by \textbf{23$\times$--77$\times$} across $1.63$--$3.77$~bpp (e.g., $684.46 \to \mathbf{21.62}$ at $3.77$~bpp; $4{,}050.95 \to \mathbf{60.35}$ at $2.18$~bpp).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Generality (MNIST MLPs):} Without architecture-specific tuning, \texttt{dreg} reduces roughness by \textbf{84.5\%}, nearly doubles low-frequency energy ($27.87\% \to 52.84\%$), and improves aggressive low-pass truncation accuracy by \textbf{+10.72\%} absolute.
    \item \textbf{Direct Spectral C11 Micro-Kernel:} Implementing $y = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x))$ via radix-2 Makhoul FFTs in C11 (\path{c_spectral_matvec_direct}) avoids $M \times N$ materialization, cutting TinyStories-10M FFN working SRAM from $8{,}448$~KB to \textbf{13.0~KB} (\textbf{650$\times$}), running \textbf{$\sim 45\times$ faster} than full-matrix 2D-IDCT, and matching dense PyTorch to $8.9 \times 10^{-7}$ max error.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Low-Bit Quantization for Language Models}
Post-training quantization (PTQ) has progressed rapidly to reduce LLM memory footprints. Uniform integer schemes (INT8 and INT4) are widely deployed in production runtimes~\citep{dettmers2022llmint8,xiao2023smoothquant}. Second-order error compensation was scaled to billion-parameter Transformers by GPTQ~\citep{frantar2022gptq}, while AWQ~\citep{lin2023awq} protects salient activation channels and SqueezeLLM~\citep{kim2023squeezellm} combines sensitivity-aware non-uniform clustering with sparse outlier extraction.

At the $\le 2$~bpp frontier, QuIP~\citep{chee2023quip} and QuIP\#~\citep{tseng2024quipsharp} apply randomized orthogonal/Hadamard transforms to enforce incoherence before 2-bit scalar or $E_8$ lattice quantization. Complementary PTQ methods---SpQR~\citep{dettmers2023spqr}, OmniQuant~\citep{shao2024omniquant}, BiLLM~\citep{huang2024billm}, and PB-LLM~\citep{shang2024pbllm}---push toward 1--2~bpp via sparse outliers, learned clipping, or partial binarization, while QAT frameworks such as BitNet b1.58~\citep{ma2024bitnet} train ternary weights end-to-end. Notably, incoherence methods like QuIP\# \emph{destroy} spatial correlation to flatten weight distributions, whereas our work exploits the complementary geometric opposite: \emph{inducing} 2D spatial smoothness so that weights compact naturally in the orthonormal 2D-DCT domain.

\subsection{Topographic Mappings in Neuroscience and Machine Learning}
Biological cortical topography was classically characterized in orientation columns~\citep{hubel1962receptive}, modeled computationally via Self-Organizing Maps~\citep{kohonen1982self}, and analyzed as wiring-length-minimizing dimension reduction~\citep{durbin1990dimension}. In deep learning, Topographic VAEs~\citep{keller2021topographic} learn equivariant capsules on 2D grids, and spectral-norm regularization~\citep{yoshida2017spectral} constrains singular values for generalization, but neither connects 2D weight-lattice smoothness to DCT energy compaction and frequency-banded quantization.

\subsection{Spectral Transforms in Deep Learning}
The Discrete Cosine Transform~\citep{ahmed1974dct} underpins JPEG~\citep{wallace1992jpeg} due to its orthonormality and near-optimal energy compaction for Markov-correlated signals~\citep{strang1999dct}. Gaussian-process theory~\citep{rasmussen2006gp} likewise predicts power-law spectral decay under Laplacian priors. In deep learning, Fourier-domain parameterizations have recently been applied to parameter-efficient fine-tuning~\citep[FourierFT;][]{gao2024fourierft}. Applying DCT directly to standard neural network weights, however, yields poor compression because standard weights have flat spectra; our work establishes 2D Dirichlet regularization as a lightweight inductive bias that makes dense weight matrices natively DCT-compressible.

\section{Methodology: Riemannian Dirichlet Regularization \& Optimal TritQ}
\label{sec:method}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{2D Dirichlet Harmonic Energy and the Layer-Scaling Pathology}
Standard regularized training minimizes a task loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}$ under $L_2$ weight decay $\frac{\gamma}{2}\|W\|_F^2$. Because $\| \pi(W)\sigma(W)\|_F^2 = \|W\|_F^2$ for arbitrary row/column permutations $(\pi, \sigma)$, standard optimization enforces zero spatial coupling between adjacent entries $W_{i,j}$ and $W_{i,j+1}$.

Treating each 2D weight matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ as a scalar field on the unit-spacing lattice $\mathbb{Z}_M \times \mathbb{Z}_N$ (avoiding aspect-ratio distortion factors $M/N$ and $N/M$), the discrete Dirichlet sum of squared edge differences is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}(W) = \sum_{i=1}^{M-1} \sum_{j=1}^N (W_{i+1, j} - W_{i, j})^2 + \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} (W_{i, j+1} - W_{i, j})^2
    = \operatorname{Tr}(W^\top L_M W) + \operatorname{Tr}(W L_N W^\top),
    \label{eq:dirichlet_discrete}
\end{equation}
where $L_M \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$ and $L_N \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ are the 1D Neumann path-graph Laplacians.

\paragraph{Diagnosing FFN Starvation under Naive Normalization.}
Consider normalizing each matrix by element count $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{numel}}(W) = \mathcal{D}(W)/(MN)$ (or edge count $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{edges}}(W) = \mathcal{D}(W)/[(M-1)N + M(N-1)] \approx \mathcal{D}(W)/(2MN)$) and averaging uniformly across $L$ layers: $\frac{\lambda}{L}\sum_{l=1}^L \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{numel}}(W^{(l)})$. Two pathologies immediately arise:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rectangular Layer Attenuation ($4\times$ FFN Starvation):} The per-weight Dirichlet gradient scales as $-\frac{2\lambda}{L M_l N_l} [\Delta W^{(l)}]_{i,j}$. Consequently, a $256 \times 1024$ SwiGLU FFN matrix receives a \textbf{4$\times$ weaker} smoothing gradient per entry than a $256 \times 256$ Attention projection. As confirmed directly in Figure~\ref{fig:spectra} (purple dash-dot curve), legacy \texttt{numel+uniform} training compacts Attention layers to $54.5\%$ energy below $\rho=0.40$ while starving FFN layers at $21.7\%$---even though FFNs contain $75\%$ of the model's linear parameters.
    \item \textbf{Scale Sensitivity and $L_2$-Like Shrinkage:} Because $\mathcal{D}(cW) = c^2 \mathcal{D}(W)$, unnormalized penalties scale with weight variance $\sigma_l^2$ and can be reduced trivially by shrinking non-DC weight magnitudes toward zero.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scale-Invariant Riemannian Rayleigh Normalization \& Parameter Weighting}
To solve both pathologies simultaneously, we introduce the \textbf{variance-normalized 2D Rayleigh quotient} of the Neumann graph Laplacian:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}(W) = \frac{\mathcal{D}(W)}{2\|W\|_F^2 + \epsilon} \in [0, 4],
    \label{eq:rayleigh}
\end{equation}
combined with \textbf{parameter-weighted layer aggregation} across all $L$ regularized linear projections $\mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}} = \{W^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^L$ (explicitly excluding embeddings and \texttt{lm\_head}, whose token indices have no spatial topology):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\mathcal{W}; \mathcal{D}) + \lambda_{\text{topo}} \sum_{l=1}^L \frac{M_l N_l}{N_{\text{total}}} \, \mathcal{R}(W^{(l)}), \quad N_{\text{total}} = \sum_{j=1}^L M_j N_j.
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
Observe how the $M_l N_l$ factor cancels $\|W^{(l)}\|_F^2 = M_l N_l \sigma_l^2$ identically:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial W^{(l)}} \left[ \frac{M_l N_l}{N_{\text{total}}} \mathcal{R}(W^{(l)}) \right]
    = \frac{1}{N_{\text{total}}\,\sigma_l^2} \Big( -\Delta W^{(l)} - \mathcal{R}(W^{(l)})\,W^{(l)} \Big).
    \label{eq:riemannian_grad}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:riemannian_grad} yields four critical properties:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exact Shape Symmetry:} Every weight in both $256\times256$ Attention and $256\times1024$ FFN matrices receives identical per-weight smoothing strength ($1 / (N_{\text{total}}\sigma_l^2)$), completely eliminating FFN starvation (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}, teal curves: $94.1\%$ FFN and $91.4\%$ Attention).
    \item \textbf{Riemannian Tangency to the Frobenius Sphere:} Because $\mathcal{R}(cW) = \mathcal{R}(W)$ is homogeneous of degree zero, Euler's theorem gives $\langle W, \nabla_W \mathcal{R}(W) \rangle = 0$ identically. The gradient is purely tangential to the Frobenius sphere $\|W\|_F = \text{const}$, preventing weight-norm collapse during from-scratch training.
    \item \textbf{Stop-Gradient Variant (\texttt{rayleigh\_sg}) for Pre-Trained Outlier Damping:} Detaching the denominator, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{sg}}(W) = \mathcal{D}(W) / (2\,\operatorname{sg}(\|W\|_F^2) + \epsilon)$, removes the $-\mathcal{R}(W)W$ radial term, yielding pure scale-invariant Neumann diffusion $\nabla_W \mathcal{R}_{\text{sg}} = -\Delta W / \|W\|_F^2$ with identically zero DC gradient ($\sum_{i,j} [\nabla_W \mathcal{R}_{\text{sg}}]_{i,j} = 0$). As we prove empirically in \S\ref{sec:ablation_geometry} and \S\ref{sec:gpt2}, full Riemannian \texttt{rayleigh} is optimal when training from scratch (preserving norm), whereas \texttt{rayleigh\_sg} excels when adapting pre-trained LLMs (GPT-2 124M) whose weights begin with oversized high-frequency outliers.
    \item \textbf{AdamW Parameter-Count Scaling Law:} From~\eqref{eq:riemannian_grad}, the per-weight Dirichlet gradient magnitude scales as $\lambda_{\text{topo}} / (N_{\text{total}} \sigma_W)$. Consequently, transferring coupling strength from a small model ($N_1, \sigma_1$) to a larger pre-trained LLM ($N_2, \sigma_2$) requires scaling $\lambda_{\text{topo}} \propto N_{\text{total}} \sigma_W$---a prediction we verify quantitatively on GPT-2 124M ($N = 84.9$M, $\sigma \approx 0.14$, $\sim 56\times$ scale shift relative to TinyStories-10M).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact Diagonalization in the 2D-DCT Basis}
The eigenvectors of the 1D Neumann path-graph Laplacian $L_N$ are precisely the orthonormal Type-II Discrete Cosine Transform basis vectors~\citep{strang1999dct}:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_k(n) = \alpha_k \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi (2n+1) k}{2N}\right), \quad \alpha_k = \begin{cases} \sqrt{1/N}, & k = 0, \\ \sqrt{2/N}, & k = 1, \dots, N-1. \end{cases}
    \label{eq:dct_basis}
\end{equation}
By tensor product, $S = D_M W D_N^\top$ and $W = D_M^\top S D_N$ with $D_M^\top D_M = I_M$, $D_N^\top D_N = I_N$ (computed in \texttt{float64} angles before casting to eliminate float32 phase drift at $N \ge 1024$). By Parseval's theorem, $\|W\|_F^2 = \|S\|_F^2$, while the Rayleigh quotient diagonalizes exactly:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}(W) = \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \mu_{k, l} S_{k, l}^2}{2 \sum_{k,l} S_{k,l}^2}, \quad \mu_{k, l} = 4 \left[ \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{2M}\right) + \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi l}{2N}\right) \right].
    \label{eq:spectral_eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
Because $\mu_{k,l} \approx \pi^2 \big((k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2\big)$ grows quadratically with radial frequency $\rho(k,l) = \sqrt{(k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2}$, minimizing $\mathcal{R}(W)$ acts as a quadratic low-pass filter on the normalized spectral energy distribution $S_{k,l}^2 / \|S\|_F^2$, connecting our objective to Laplacian eigenmaps~\citep{belkin2003laplacian} and Gaussian-process smoothness priors~\citep{rasmussen2006gp}.

\subsection{Hierarchical Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq}) with Zero-Overhead Optimal Scaling}
\label{sec:tritq}
Partitioning the 2D frequency grid by normalized radius $\rho(u, v) = \sqrt{(k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2} \in [0, \sqrt{2})$ via cutoffs $0 \le r_0 \le r_1 \le r_2$, we allocate bit-depth according to spectral importance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Band 0 ($\rho \le r_0$, $8.0$~bpp):} DC and basal harmonics, quantized to 8-bit unsigned integers (\texttt{uint8}).
    \item \textbf{Band 1 ($r_0 < \rho \le r_1$, $4.0$~bpp):} Mid-frequency harmonics, quantized to 4-bit nibbles packed 2 per byte.
    \item \textbf{Band 2 ($r_1 < \rho \le r_2$, $1.60$~bpp):} High-mid harmonics, quantized to balanced ternary trits $t \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ packed 5 per byte via exact Base-3 arithmetic ($3^5 = 243 \le 256$):
    \begin{equation}
        B = \sum_{p=0}^{4} 3^p (t_p + 1) \in [0, 242], \quad \text{Bitrate}_{\text{trit}} = \frac{8}{5} = 1.60 \text{ bits/trit},
    \end{equation}
    saving $20\%$ storage vs.\ 2-bit encoding and unpacking in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ via a $1.25$~KB L1-cache lookup table.
    \item \textbf{Band 3 ($\rho > r_2$, $0.0$~bpp):} High-frequency tail, truncated to zero.
\end{itemize}
Non-topographic parameters (embeddings, \texttt{lm\_head}, LayerNorms) are stored uncompressed in FP16 so vocabulary geometry is never distorted.

\paragraph{Two Zero-Bit-Overhead Quantizer Optimizations.}
When Dirichlet regularization concentrates $>90\%$ of energy into Bands~0 and~1, the spectral distribution becomes strongly heavy-tailed, causing naive \texttt{min}--\texttt{max} scaling and heuristic ternary scales to suffer severe dynamic-range loss. We introduce two optimal scalar upgrades that keep the exact \texttt{.tritq} v1 binary layout and C11 decoder completely unchanged:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{1D Lloyd--Max Band-2 Ternary Quantization (\texttt{band2\_mode="lloyd\_max"}):} Rather than heuristic $\tau = 0.5\sigma_2, \alpha = \sigma_2$ (which can overshoot Band-2 energy on heavy-tailed spectra), we run 3 iterations of 1D symmetric Lloyd--Max fixed-point updates:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha^{(k+1)} = \mathbb{E}\big[|s| \;\big|\; |s| > \tau^{(k)}\big], \quad \tau^{(k+1)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{(k+1)},
    \end{equation}
    strictly minimizing Band-2 MSE and guaranteeing reconstructed energy $\le 100\%$.
    \item \textbf{MSE-Optimal Asymmetric Clipping on Bands~0 \& 1 (\texttt{band01\_mode="mse\_clip"}):} For Band~0 (256 levels) and especially Band~1 (only 16 levels), a single large near-DC harmonic stretches unclipped $[\min, \max]$ and collapses resolution on thousands of remaining modes. We evaluate a lightweight quantile grid (including one-sided trims for asymmetric DC spikes and always including full $[\min, \max]$) and pick the affine range $[\text{lo}^*, \text{hi}^*]$ minimizing $\|s - \hat{s}\|_2^2$. This guarantees MSE $\le$ unclipped \texttt{min}--\texttt{max} at \textbf{zero extra bits}.
\end{enumerate}
As verified in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, these two upgrades alone slash $1.638$~bpp perplexity on converged models by \textbf{3.06$\times$} ($51.55 \to 16.87$~PPL).

\paragraph{Direct Spectral-Domain Matrix-Vector Product.}
Explicitly materializing a wide FFN weight matrix $W = D_M^\top S D_N$ (e.g., $1024 \times 256$) requires $8{,}448$~KB of working buffers and dense $\mathcal{O}(M N (M+N))$ IDCT FLOPs. Crucially, during single-token autoregressive decoding ($B=1, T=1$), re-associating the product avoids full-matrix reconstruction entirely:
\begin{equation}
    y = x W^\top = x (D_N^\top S^\top D_M) = \big((x D_N^\top) S^\top\big) D_M = \operatorname{IDCT}_{1D}\!\Big(S \cdot \operatorname{DCT}_{1D}(x)\Big).
    \label{eq:spectral_matvec}
\end{equation}
We implement~\eqref{eq:spectral_matvec} directly in C11 (\texttt{c\_spectral\_matvec\_direct}) using radix-2 $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$ Makhoul 1D DCT-II/IDCT-II transforms with \texttt{double} twiddle recurrences, streaming compressed Band~0/1/2 rows on the fly with \emph{zero} $M \times N$ buffers and \emph{zero} stored basis matrices. On a TinyStories-10M FFN layer ($1024 \times 256$ at $1.637$~bpp), \texttt{c\_spectral\_matvec\_direct} cuts working SRAM from $8{,}448$~KB to \textbf{13.0~KB} (\textbf{650$\times$} reduction), runs \textbf{45.6$\times$ faster} than full-matrix 2D-IDCT reconstruction ($363.7\,\mu\text{s}$ vs.\ $16.6$~ms on CPU), and matches dense PyTorch \texttt{x @ W.T} to \textbf{8.9 $\times$ 10$^{-7}$} max absolute error.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Riemannian Rayleigh Dirichlet Training \& Optimal Base-3 Trit Quantization (\texttt{.tritq})}
\label{alg:dreg_pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Dataset $\mathcal{D}$, linear projections $\mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}} = \{W^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^L$ (excluding embeddings/\texttt{lm\_head}), coupling $\lambda_0$, cooldown ratio $\rho_c$ (default $0.15$), radial cutoffs $(r_0, r_1, r_2)$.
\STATE \textbf{Phase 1: Scale-Invariant Parameter-Weighted Harmonic Training}
\FOR{step $t = 1$ \TO $T$}
    \STATE $\lambda(t) \leftarrow \lambda_0$ if $t < (1 - \rho_c) T$ else $0$ \quad (\texttt{StepCooldownScheduler}).
    \STATE Compute task loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(X, Y; \mathcal{W})$ and $N_{\text{total}} = \sum_{l=1}^L M_l N_l$.
    \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{\text{topo}} \leftarrow \lambda(t) \sum_{l=1}^L \frac{M_l N_l}{N_{\text{total}}} \frac{\|\Delta_x W^{(l)}\|_F^2 + \|\Delta_y W^{(l)}\|_F^2}{2\|W^{(l)}\|_F^2 + \epsilon}$ \quad (\texttt{rayleigh}, or $\operatorname{sg}(\|W\|_F^2)$ for pre-trained LLMs).
    \STATE Update $\mathcal{W} \leftarrow \text{AdamW}(\mathcal{W}, \nabla_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{topo}}), \eta)$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Phase 2: Zero-Overhead Optimal Base-3 Spectral Quantization}
\FOR{each $W \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{lin}}$}
    \STATE $S \leftarrow D_M W D_N^\top$ (2D-DCT); compute $\rho(k, l) = \sqrt{(k/M)^2 + (l/N)^2}$.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 0 ($\rho \le r_0$):} MSE-optimal clipped 8-bit \texttt{uint8} quantization.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 1 ($r_0 < \rho \le r_1$):} MSE-optimal clipped 4-bit nibble packing (2 values/byte).
    \STATE \textbf{Band 2 ($r_1 < \rho \le r_2$):} 3-step 1D Lloyd--Max ternary trits packed 5/byte via $B = \sum_{p=0}^4 3^p (t_p + 1)$.
    \STATE \textbf{Band 3 ($\rho > r_2$):} Truncate to zero (0 bits stored).
\ENDFOR
\STATE Serialize non-topographic parameters (embeddings, \texttt{lm\_head}, norms) in FP16.
\RETURN Compressed \texttt{.tritq} checkpoint (exact v1 binary format).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Broader Impact}
\label{sec:discussion}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Intrinsic Self-Organization vs.\ External Clamping.}
During early exploratory experiments, forcing cross-seed alignment via an external sinusoidal potential $R_{\text{ext}}(W) = -\lambda \sum \sin(2\pi i / M) W_{i,j}$ produced $\rho_{\text{raw}} = 0.996$ correlation across seeds but triggered a catastrophic \textbf{rank-1 clamping attractor} (effective singular rank collapsed from $199.2$ to $3.58$). Intrinsic Dirichlet diffusion avoids this degeneracy entirely by penalizing local gradients rather than pinning weights to a fixed template.

\paragraph{Edge Hardware Implications.}
By combining \texttt{.tritq} Base-3 packing ($1.72$~MB for all 56 linear matrices of TinyStories-10M at $1.638$~bpp) with our C11 direct spectral matvec (\texttt{c\_spectral\_matvec\_direct}, \textbf{13.0~KB} working SRAM, $650\times$ smaller than explicit 2D-IDCT materialization, $45.6\times$ faster), sub-2-bit spectral inference becomes feasible on commodity $512$~KB--$1$~MB microcontrollers (e.g., STM32H7, ESP32-S3, Cortex-M55) without full-layer scratchpads.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Four limitations remain transparent opportunities for future work:
(1) \textbf{FP32 Capacity Trade-off:} Strong Rayleigh smoothing ($\lambda = 2.0$, annealed) raises converged TinyStories-10M FP32 PPL from $5.81$ to $7.78$; exploring cosine-annealed schedules, spectral-QAT fine-tuning, or layer-adaptive $\lambda_l$ may narrow this dense gap further while preserving sub-2-bit gains.
(2) \textbf{Second-Order PTQ & Billion-Scale QAT Comparisons:} While we now benchmark against group-wise INT2 ($g=64$) and budget-matched quantized SVD, head-to-head comparisons against Hessian-based PTQ (GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP\#) and native QAT (BitNet b1.58) at 1B+ parameter scales remain important future work.
(3) \textbf{Pre-Trained LLM Residual Discontinuities:} In GPT-2 124M, concatenated $[W_Q \mid W_K \mid W_V]$ matrices (\texttt{c\_attn}) introduce sharp internal step boundaries across head blocks, and short 1,500-step adaptation still leaves a gap below $2.0$~bpp ($178.7$~PPL at $1.63$~bpp); splitting $Q/K/V$ before smoothing or combining \texttt{dreg} with spectral PEFT~\citep{gao2024fourierft} is a natural next step.
(4) \textbf{Planar Lattice Assumption:} Our operator uses a planar 2D Neumann grid; toroidal, spherical, or learned graph-Laplacian topologies remain unexplored.

\paragraph{Reproducibility \& Broader Impact.}
All library code (\texttt{dreg}, installable via \texttt{pip install dirichlet-regularization}), the C11 DMA and direct spectral matvec micro-kernel (\path{kernel/}), 102 automated tests, CPU reproduction scripts (\path{examples/reproduce_all.py}), Modal multi-seed training/evaluation pipelines (\path{benchmarks/}), and raw JSON metrics (\path{results/}) are released under the MIT license at \url{https://github.com/mcarbonell/dirichlet-regularization}. Lowering storage and SRAM requirements enables private offline edge AI with lower energy consumption; because aggressive compression can shift calibration and error profiles, compressed checkpoints should be audited before safety-critical deployment.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented Dirichlet Weight Regularization (\texttt{dreg}), a training-time geometric inductive bias that breaks permutation invariance in neural weight matrices to induce smooth, 2D-DCT-compressible weight manifolds. By identifying and resolving FFN gradient starvation via scale-invariant Riemannian Rayleigh normalization and parameter-weighted aggregation, and pairing it with zero-overhead MSE-clipped / Lloyd--Max Base-3 Trit quantization (\texttt{.tritq}) and controlled annealing, we achieve \textbf{4.04$\times$} lower perplexity at $1.638$~bpp ($16.87$ vs.\ $68.21$), \textbf{11.4$\times$} lower perplexity at sub-1-bit $0.914$~bpp ($95.60$ vs.\ $1{,}085.5$), \textbf{2.19$\times$--3.45$\times$} improvements over group-wise INT2 ($g=64$), \textbf{23$\times$--77$\times$} perplexity reductions on pre-trained GPT-2 (124M), and a \textbf{650$\times$} working-SRAM reduction via direct spectral C11 inference. Together, these results establish spatial weight geometry as a high-leverage axis for compact neural network design.

\section{Continuous Gradient Flow \& Riemannian Sphere Derivation}
\label{sec:appendix_derivation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let $\mathcal{W}: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ denote a smooth scalar field over a compact domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2$. The continuous Dirichlet energy $\mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{W}) = \frac{1}{2}\int_\Omega \|\nabla \mathcal{W}\|^2\,du\,dv$ has first variation under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions ($\nabla \mathcal{W}\cdot\hat{n}=0$ on $\partial\Omega$):
\begin{equation}
    \left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon} \mathcal{E}_D^{\text{cont}}(\mathcal{W} + \epsilon \eta)\right|_{\epsilon=0} = -\int_\Omega \eta\,\Delta \mathcal{W}\,du\,dv,
\end{equation}
yielding the heat diffusion flow $\partial_t \mathcal{W} = \Delta \mathcal{W}$. On the discrete lattice $\mathbb{Z}_M \times \mathbb{Z}_N$, minimizing the scale-invariant Rayleigh quotient $\mathcal{R}(W) = \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(W)/\|W\|_F^2$ yields the gradient
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_W \mathcal{R}(W) = \frac{1}{\|W\|_F^2}\Big(-\Delta W - \mathcal{R}(W)\,W\Big) = \left(I - \frac{W \otimes W}{\|W\|_F^2}\right)\left(\frac{-\Delta W}{\|W\|_F^2}\right),
\end{equation}
which is the exact orthogonal projection of the Neumann diffusion vector $-\Delta W / \|W\|_F^2$ onto the tangent space $T_W \mathbb{S}^{MN-1}$ of the Frobenius sphere. Consequently, $\langle W, \nabla_W \mathcal{R}(W) \rangle = 0$ identically, preserving weight scale while driving high spatial frequencies to zero.
